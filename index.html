EEG State Simulator

A generator of multichannel EEG for arousal states.

This is a model, not a measurement. No pixel here is data recorded from a brain.

The trace scrolls: new signal arrives at the right-hand edge and older signal leaves at the left, as on a paper chart. Drag the slider to scrub — that pauses. Changing µV/mm is not autoscaling: the scale is stated, and it is the same for every channel and every state, so the amplitude difference between N3 delta and waking alpha survives.

The pink bands mark injected events — the ground-truth spindles, K-complexes and slow oscillations, at the exact times the generator placed them. Knowing where they are is the thing no real recording can give you, which is what the gates use them for. mains adds 60 Hz interference with a per-channel phase and gain, so it cannot be removed by changing the reference.

Below the heavier rule sit respiration (belt displacement) and ECG. They are not EEG and are not in the montage: each is scaled to its own lane and states its full-scale range, because an ECG R wave is around 1000 µV and would be 14 cm tall at the scale above. Watch the heart rate rise on inspiration and fall on expiration — that is respiratory sinus arrhythmia, driven from the same respiratory phase that drives the EEG coupling in Demo 1.

Referencing is a linear operator on the data, not a display preference — average reference removes exactly one dimension by construction, and a Laplacian is a spatial high-pass. Watch the effective rank below change as you switch.

Filter panel

Standard practice high-passes at 0.5–1 Hz — directly on top of the respiratory rate.

Causal is what an acquisition system actually does, and it distorts phase near the cutoff.

Alternatives to high-pass filtering exist — robust detrending (de Cheveigné & Arzounian) fits and removes a slow polynomial instead. It is a Tier 1 addition. High-pass filtering trades a known artifact for a known distortion; it is not a mistake.

A naive coupling number is mostly artifact. The filter removes it, correctly — (a) is an artifact. The physiological coupling (c) was never what the number was reporting.

How each row is computed

(a) is the component locked to respiratory phase, in µV. “of injected” compares it against ground truth at the electrode — the source amplitude times the gain of projection and of the reference you picked, both linear and both known exactly. Charging that geometry to the filter would be the same conflation this demo is about.

Noise floor is the same estimator run off-resonance, where nothing was injected. It is not zero, so compare (a) against the floor rather than against zero.

(c) is named by mechanism, not quantity. It reads χ̂ at the respiratory rate, where both halves of mechanism (c) can appear, in true χ units. It is a control that does not move with the filter — not a clean measurement of exponent modulation, since the shipped depth sits only ~3× above the floor.

Ground truth exists here only because the generator injected it; no real dataset can show this comparison. G4 runs it on a 300 s record because a 30 s window cannot separate the modulation from its sidebands.

Effective rank is the participation ratio of the channel covariance — a threshold-free count of how many independent things 19 electrodes are actually carrying. It is far below 19, and it should be: volume conduction guarantees that. But a value near 1 would mean every channel is the same trace scaled, which no recording looks like.

The two exponents are different quantities, not two estimates of one. Comparing them is a type error in the source.
